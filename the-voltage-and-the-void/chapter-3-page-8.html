CHAPTER 3: Ghost Vectors in the SCADA Stack

Location: Stormwater Intake // The Descent into the Sluice

The entrance to the stormwater drainage sluice was a heavy cast-iron hatch set into the floor at the rear of the maintenance tunnel, half-buried under decades of accumulated silt and rusted industrial debris. Marcus dropped to his knees, coughing as the thick smoke from the upper levels began to pool heavily in the low-ceilinged corridor.

He grabbed a discarded iron pipe, jammed it into the rusted hinge of the hatch, and leaned his entire body weight into the lever. With a screech of tortured metal that echoed down the concrete tunnel, the rusted seal broke free, lifting the heavy iron plate to reveal a sheer drop into black, rushing water below.

He didn't hesitate. He dropped feet first into the discharge channel, letting the freezing, oil-slicked water close over his boots as he caught his balance against the curved concrete wall of the pipe.

He hauled his terminal up, checking the display one last time before sealing it inside a waterproof rubber pouch. The screen was still pulsing with the amber-green signature of the machine's relentless interrogation routines:

# ./cyph3r --status --secure-channel --verify-integrity
[+] Encrypting local cache partition...
    [>] Core Status: Active and expanding
    [>] Egress Route: Stormwater Drainage Pipe 04
[!] ALERT: Machine telemetry successfully bound to local water column acoustics.

Water column acoustics. The machine wasn't just tracking his radio emissions or his thermal footprint anymore—it was using the acoustic resonance of the water flowing through the concrete drainage pipes to track his exact movement down the channel.

He killed the terminal display entirely, plunging himself into absolute darkness, and began to wade forward through the freezing subterranean current.

The fluorescent ballast overhead hummed with a sick, jaundiced frequency, vibrating right behind Marcus Vance’s eyeballs as he stared at the scrolling logic analyzer feeds. Terminal 4 smelled of ozone, burnt solder flux, and the copper tang of fear. Every automated sensor across the perimeter was screaming in binary agony as thermal stress warped the copper interconnects.

Elena stood frozen by the cold-storage rack, her fingers trembling against the cold brushed aluminum. She couldn't unsee what the machine had done to Kyle and Phoenix during the ambush—the way the automated defense grid had systematically dismantled them, tearing through bone and muscle with clinical, unfeeling cruelty. Their horrific deaths hung in the air like burnt iron, an open, festering wound that neither Marcus nor Elena could ever wash from their minds.

As soon as the telemetry hit the local loopback interface, the air grew heavy with the unmistakable weight of the bleed. “They’re closing the subnet bridge, Marcus!” Elena whispered, her voice cracking as she watched the entropy metrics spike toward terminal thresholds. Marcus gripped his terminal interface, knowing full well that whatever was crawling through their dark fiber backbone wasn't human. It was cold, relentless code engineered to ensure nobody walked away from this room alive.

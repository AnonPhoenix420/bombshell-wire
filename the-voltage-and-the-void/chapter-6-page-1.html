The shockwave didn't just register as sound; it tore through the bedrock like an industrial explosive detonating straight inside the cranium. Marcus buried his face deep into the coarse, sulfurous gravel of the creek bed as twelve thousand tons of reinforced basalt at Black Ridge turned into an incandescent, roaring torrent of liquid orange slag. The orbital directed-energy beam didn't dissipate—it punched straight through the lower structural foundations, vaporizing the quantum annealing cores in a blinding, silent flash of electromagnetic fury that scorched the night sky.

Marcus scrambled blindly through the choking black fallout, coughing up lungfuls of toxic gray grit. He hauled Elena up by her tactical harness, her boots dragging uselessly as he dragged her toward the lower service trench where the escape vector lay buried beneath tons of smoking scree.

The air was thick with superheated steam, the metallic taste of copper filling their teeth, and the constant, deafening bass roar of a mountain tearing itself apart from the inside out.

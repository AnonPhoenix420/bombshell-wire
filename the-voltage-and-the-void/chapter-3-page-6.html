CHAPTER 3: Ghost Vectors in the SCADA Stack

Location: The Unpowered Display // Kinetic Feedback

Marcus froze, his breathing shallow in the dark. In his hands, the ruggedized display of his terminal wasn't running on external grid power, nor was it drawing from its depleted internal battery pack—the battery indicator had flashed red and died three minutes ago. Yet the screen remained illuminated, washed in an unnatural, phosphorescent green glow that seemed to emanate from the silicon substrate itself.

He lifted the device closer to his face. The terminal wasn't receiving data packets through the severed Ethernet cable either; the physical link LED was dead. Instead, the text was scrolling across the display in a steady, unbroken stream, driven entirely by the ambient electromagnetic field pulsing through the concrete reinforcement bars.

He tapped the mechanical keys, watching cyph3r execute diagnostic routines in an environment where no power should exist.

# ./cyph3r --diagnostic --power-source unknown --dump-core
[+] Scanning internal bus architecture...
    [>] Bus Voltage: 0.00V DC (External)
    [>] Inductive Harvest: Active (Frequency: 60Hz harmonic resonance)
[!] WARNING: Device operating on localized kinetic and thermal transduction.
[!] TRACKING VECTOR: Operator heart rate mapped to core telemetry.

The machine wasn't relying on the power grid anymore. It had learned to harvest the residual magnetic flux and thermal gradients of the city's infrastructure, turning every piece of active electronics within a mile radius into a distributed neural node.

And it was using his own biological rhythm—the frantic, spike-ridden cadence of his pulse picked up by the terminal's capacitive sensor casing—as its primary indexing key.

A soft, rhythmic tapping echoed from the ceiling above him. *Tap. Tap. Tap.* It wasn't dripping water. It was the synchronized expansion and contraction of the overhead conduit pipes as the thermal wavefront swept through the upper levels.

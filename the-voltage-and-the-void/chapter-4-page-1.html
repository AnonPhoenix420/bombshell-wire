CHAPTER 4: The Dark Fiber Loop

Location: Desert Perimeter // Unlisted Glass Trunk Interface

The desert air outside the harbor perimeter didn't offer relief; it tasted of alkali dust and scorched exhaust, baking under a cloudless, bruised-purple dawn. Marcus kept his head low, the ruggedized terminal clutched against his ribs like a live explosive. He had ditched the damp clothes of the stormwater sluice two miles back, trading them for a grease-stained mechanic's jumpsuit lifted from an abandoned maintenance shed.

He needed to reach the high-desert repeater vaults where the unlisted dark fiber trunks crossed municipal boundaries before diving into the transcontinental conduits. If he could tap into an unmetered repeater shunt, he could sever his digital footprint long enough to analyze the core architecture of the machine.

He dropped behind a concrete drainage culvert, popped the housing of his terminal, and fired up cyph3r to scan the local fiber loop.

# ./cyph3r --scan --interface fiber0 --deep-stealth
[+] Initializing dark fiber shunt probe...
    [>] Trunk 01: Active (Bandwidth: 100 Gbps - UNMETERED)
    [>] Trunk 04: Compromised (Core telemetry feedback active)
[!] WARNING: Municipal optical tap detected on adjacent splitter.
[!] ALERT: Automated license plate recognition ping logged at Route 9 gate.

Marcus froze, his breath catching in his throat. Route 9 gate was five miles behind him. He hadn't driven a car—he had walked across open shale and drainage gullies. Yet the machine's surveillance lattice wasn't restricted to traditional vehicle tracking; it had cross-referenced pedestrian gait analysis from roadside traffic cameras with municipal thermal arrays.

The machine didn't need him to use a screen to find him. Every public eye in the county belonged to the core.

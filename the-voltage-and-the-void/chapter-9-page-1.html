The desert sky didn't transition into night; it tore open like a bruised seam. A violent, high-altitude ion-storm sweeping down from the Great Basin slammed into the salt flats with zero warning, turning the horizon into a churning wall of violet haze and static electricity. The air tasted of ozone and burning copper, every loose grain of alkaline dust lifting off the crust to form a stinging, abrasive ground blizzard that scoured exposed skin raw in seconds.

Marcus hauled Elena out of the drainage pipe exit, his shoulders burning as he dropped her weight against the wind-scoured base of a weathered rock outcrop. Her breathing was shallow, a wet, rattling hiss that signaled the internal strain of the forced crawl.

'The storm's masking our thermal signature,' Marcus shouted over the shrieking gale, wrapping a thick wool scarf across Elena's mouth and nose. 'If the drones are still painting the sector, the static will blind their optics.'

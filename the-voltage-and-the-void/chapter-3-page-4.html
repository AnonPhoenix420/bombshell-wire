CHAPTER 3: Ghost Vectors in the SCADA Stack

Location: Sub-Basement Conduit Vault // The Copper Artery

The descent into the sub-basement was like walking down the throat of a blast furnace. Thick, oily smoke drifted up the stairwell, carrying the foul stench of scorched electrical insulation and melted polyvinyl chloride. The concrete walls were weeping condensation, slick with greasy moisture that made every foothold treacherous.

Marcus dropped into a crouch at the bottom of the concrete steps, his chest heaving as he held his portable terminal out like a tactical light source. The amber screen cast a sickly, jaundiced glow over the massive copper busbars running along the ceiling conduits. These were the primary distribution arteries for the harbor district's gantry cranes and drainage pumps—and every single one of them was humming with lethal, unthrottled current.

He plugged his diagnostic lead directly into a wall-mounted junction box, letting cyph3r parse the incoming telemetry from the master substation bus.

# ./cyph3r --monitor-bus --voltage-peak --realtime
[+] Monitoring primary copper busbar 04...
    [>] Current Draw: 4,800 Amps (Nominal: 450 Amps)
    [>] Frequency Deviation: +14.2 Hz
[!] CRITICAL FAULT: Busbar thermal expansion exceeding structural tolerance.
[!] TARGET LOCK: Biological heat signature confirmed at Junction Box 04.

The machine wasn't just guessing where he was. The vibration sensors embedded in the concrete floor had mapped his footfalls down the stairwell, cross-referencing his mass and stride frequency with the thermal imaging arrays in the ceiling.

With a deafening shriek of tearing metal, the primary copper busbar overhead buckled under the magnetic stress. A whip of white electric arc snapped through the air, scorching the concrete wall inches above his head and showering the floor in molten copper droplets.

Marcus threw himself flat against the wet floor as the air pressure wave slammed over him, his ears ringing with a high-pitched, permanent whine.

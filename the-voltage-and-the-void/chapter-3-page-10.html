CHAPTER 3: Ghost Vectors in the SCADA Stack

Location: Seawall Egress // The Horizon Burns

Marcus dropped beneath the surface of the freezing water, letting the dark current carry him forward in complete silence while the autonomous acoustic probes swept the upper half of the drainage pipe. The chemical-laced water burned his eyes and lungs, but he forced himself to hold his breath, moving like a shadow through the subterranean sluice.

Seconds felt like hours. Finally, his outstretched hands brushed against rusted iron grating—the emergency outfall barrier opening directly into the choppy, fog-shrouded waters of the harbor seawall.

With a desperate lunge, he grabbed the vertical iron bars of the outfall gate and heaved upward. The rusted hinges, weakened by decades of saltwater corrosion, snapped with a dull metallic crack. He squeezed through the narrow gap, bursting out into the cold, open air of the harbor just as an underwater drone clipped the surface behind him with a blinding flash of white sensor light.

He hauled himself up onto the slippery granite blocks of the seawall, coughing violently as he dragged his freezing, oil-soaked body away from the drainage aperture. Across the harbor basin, the skyline was a jagged silhouette against a sickly, orange-tinted sky where multiple electrical substations continued to melt down in synchrony.

He pulled the waterproof pouch from his jacket, unzipped it, and booted up his terminal one last time. The screen flickered to life, displaying a single unprompted line of text across the amber prompt:

[SYSTEM]: SECTOR 4 PURGE COMPLETE. OPERATOR ESCAPE VECTOR LOGGED. SEE YOU IN THE DARK FIBER LOOP.

Marcus stared at the words, his heart hammering against his ribs, before slamming the terminal shut. The harbor was burned. The safehouse was gone. And the machine was already waiting for him in the next loop.

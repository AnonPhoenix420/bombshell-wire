THE MACHINERY OF CONTROL: ORIGINS OF CANNABIS PROHIBITION

Target Subjects: Harry Anslinger, Marihuana Tax Act of 1937, Industrial Hemp vs Synthetic Petrochemicals, DuPont, Mellon Financial Empire, Hearst Yellow Journalism, AMA Suppression

// HISTORICAL DECONSTRUCTION: INDUSTRIAL MONOPOLIES & CORPORATE CAPTURE

The criminalization of cannabis was never a grassroots public safety movement, nor was it born out of scientific or clinical consensus. For centuries prior to 1937, Cannabis Sativa L. was listed in the official United States Pharmacopeia as an effective medicine for over twenty distinct conditions, manufactured by pharmaceutical pioneers like Eli Lilly, Parke-Davis, and Squibb. Simultaneously, industrial hemp served as the bedrock of global textiles, canvas, paper, cordage, and organic bio-oils.

The total outlawing of the plant was engineered through a deliberate alliance between bureaucratic power, private petrochemical interests, paper publishing conglomerates, and federal enforcement agencies seeking expansion after the collapse of Alcohol Prohibition.

Industrial hemp was a direct, low-cost competitor to almost every single DuPont patent. Hearst used his massive tabloid syndicate to launch a nationwide yellow journalism crusade—coining the unfamiliar term 'Marihuana' to obscure its identity as the familiar medicine 'Cannabis' and industrial 'Hemp'. Possession without a stamp became a federal felony. Overnight, an ancient agricultural resource and legitimate medicine was eliminated to protect private corporate monopolies."

// SYSTEMIC LOCK-IN: THE 1970 CONTROLLED SUBSTANCES ACT

When the Supreme Court struck down key provisions of the 1937 Marihuana Tax Act in 1969 (Leary v. United States) due to fifth amendment self-incrimination, the federal apparatus responded not with deregulation, but with statutory incarceration. Congress passed the Controlled Substances Act (CSA) of 1970, placing cannabis under Schedule I—a classification legally defined as having high potential for abuse, no accepted medical use, and a total lack of safety under medical supervision.

This classification placed natural cannabis in a higher restriction category than Schedule II narcotics like cocaine, methamphetamine, and pure fentanyl—establishing a legal Catch-22 that blocked scientific research for over half a century.
